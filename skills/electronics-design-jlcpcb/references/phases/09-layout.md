# Phase 9: layout and 3D

Place and route the board on the frozen definition so that every datasheet rule is met, with a real 3D body for every part, and leave a saved, filled board that phase 10 can prove.

## Inputs

- The frozen `BOARD_DEFINITION.md`, `SILK_ID_BLOCK.md`, `mech/interface.json` and the outline-only board of phase 8.
- The gate 1 schematic and netlist, unchanged, and the BOM with LCSC numbers.
- `LAYOUT_RULES_LEDGER.md`: every rule gets a "how it is met" entry in this phase.
- `references/pcb-rules/general.md`, `emc.md` and the files for this board's circuit types (`power.md`, `mcu-digital.md`, `usb.md`, `rf.md`, `analog.md`, `protection-esd.md`, `dfm-dft.md`); `references/jlcpcb/capabilities.md`.
- Tools: `scripts/fetch_parts.py`, `scripts/fp_audit.py`, `scripts/kicad_io.py`, `scripts/drc_run.py` with `scripts/jlcpcb.kicad_dru`, `scripts/zone_check.py`, `scripts/neck_check.py`, `scripts/return_path_check.py`, `scripts/impedance_check.py`, `scripts/body_check.py`, `scripts/interface_export.py`, and the helpers in `scripts/layout/` (a first pass only, see `references/tools.md`; how to use them, their limits and the lessons on route order: `scripts/layout/README.md`).
- `references/lessons.md`, section "Phase 9: layout and 3D".

## Steps

1. **Audit every footprint against the maker's drawing before placing it.** Fetch JLCPCB's footprint and 3D model for each LCSC number with `scripts/fetch_parts.py`. Dump pads, drills, courtyard and 3D origin with `scripts/fp_audit.py`. Compare with the drawing: pitch, pad size, drill and slot size, pin 1, the maker's PCB-edge line. Keep one row per footprint with the drawing page.
   - The courtyard encloses the pads and the body, with margin. A courtyard that follows only the body hides overlaps from DRC.
   - Use the maker's land pattern for power inductors and fine-pitch chips.
   - One pin-1 or polarity mark, visible with the part fitted (outside the body outline). Silkscreen lines at the manufacturer's minimum or wider. No field other than the reference on the silkscreen.
   - A library footprint that you change becomes a project copy with its own name.
2. **Give every part a real 3D model:** the supplier's library first, the maker's second, one built from the drawing last. Check each model's origin and rotation against its pads, and its colours in a render. Add the mating plug of every connector for the checks. If a site blocks the fetch, give the user the link (`references/working-rules.md`).
3. **Set the net classes and the rule file.** Put every net in a class. Write the fast and sensitive nets into `[fast_nets]` of `board.toml` and every line with an impedance target into `[[impedance.line]]` (`references/pcb-rules/general.md` GEN-19), and the footprints exempt from the via-in-pad rule into `[drc.via_in_pad_exempt]` (step 13). Start from `scripts/jlcpcb.kicad_dru` and make these errors: minimum width per class on every layer, copper to edge including corner arcs, via hole to SMD pad (with the exposed-pad exemptions of step 13, by footprint name), silkscreen to pad, the courtyard rules, at least two thermal spokes. Set the rules before the first fill. A net class is a setting, not a proof: test each custom rule on a copy that contains the defect. Write the list of fast and sensitive nets into `board.toml` (`[fast_nets]`: clocks, USB and other pairs, fast bus lines, RF lines, feedback and sensor lines) now; step 18b proves their return paths against it.
   - KiCad drops the WHOLE rule file without a word when it holds one syntax error (a `#` comment after a rule on the same line is one), and then reports a clean board. `scripts/drc_run.py` plants a violation of its own to prove that the file loaded; it cannot prove a rule you added. After every edit of the rule file, plant one violation per changed rule on a copy and see it fire.
   - A silkscreen-to-pad rule with a line `(layer "?.Silkscreen")` never fires. Use the rule as the template has it.
4. **Keep the layout reproducible.** Build it from scripts kept in the project's `src/` folder, so the same inputs give the same board, and keep the board of each stage. A fix after a review is a small script that can run again, not a hand edit that the next rebuild loses.
5. **Place in this order, by the zone map of phase 8,** taking every fixed coordinate from the interface file and typing none:
   - the fixed mechanical parts: connectors, holes, parts at a wall; nothing in the free bands along slot or rail edges, on either side (bodies, courtyards, solder joints, exposed copper); an antenna at its maker's position with its keep-out free of copper on every layer, parts, screws and metal, and its three-part matching network at the feed (`references/pcb-rules/emc.md` section 6);
   - each chip's critical parts by its ledger rules: input and decoupling capacitors at their pins, feedback and sense parts; ESD and TVS parts at their connector, rail clamps and crowbars at the rail they protect, as the energy map of the design note says. Keep a decoupling table: per supply pin the capacitor, its pad-to-pin distance, the squares of copper in its loop and its ground via;
   - the rest. Keep temperature-sensitive protection parts away from hot parts, and every part away from break-away bridges and hand-solder areas.
6. **Hold the priority: electrical first, then mechanical, then silkscreen.** Make it the simplest layout that meets the rules: the fewest layers, vias and special processes that still satisfy them (`references/working-rules.md` section 12). A label never moves a critical part. Record where a label lost; do not ask the user about it.
7. **Plan label room at placement.** Place the labels before routing, keep later vias 0.1 mm away from them, and keep the information block's area free of vias and tracks. A part with no legal label spot goes on a list for phase 11.
8. **Leave one wide routing channel** between the blocks, and spread out clusters that long nets must cross. A packed cluster beside empty board once cost hours of routing.
9. **Escape dense pins by hand first:** fine-pitch chip pins and connector rows get their short stubs and vias before any router runs.
10. **Route in this order:** pours and wide power nets; a few hand-picked nets that must cross the board; the long control nets; the short nets. Run `scripts/drc_run.py` with schematic parity after every stage. Give each wide feed a planned path, so it does not close the last gap of a long net. The router of `scripts/layout/` is never given a fast or sensitive net (`references/pcb-rules/general.md` GEN-19), a pair, a length-matched or impedance-controlled line, or the escape of a fine-pitch part: it changes layers and crosses gaps in the ground freely. Draw those by hand first (`scripts/layout/copper.py`), then prove them with `scripts/return_path_check.py`. What the router leaves open, a pad without a via, a part without room or without a label are in its report: read it after every build.
10b. **Cuts need a job.** Every slot, cut-out, moat or plane split has its ledger row (job, requirement or datasheet page, proof); none is added to "separate grounds" (`references/pcb-rules/general.md`, the table before GEN-61). A moat or a plane split exists only where a chip maker's datasheet or hardware design guide asks for it expressly, with the page in the ledger, or as an isolation barrier; in every other case the plane stays whole (GEN-64). No net crosses a moat or a split except over its bridge. An isolation barrier stays empty on every layer (`references/pcb-rules/protection-esd.md` ESD-75).
11. **Keep the ground whole.** On a 2-layer board write the ground side into `planes` of the table `[zone]` in `board.toml` (for example `planes = ["B.Cu"]`): without it `scripts/zone_check.py` judges no layer as a plane and its result is UNKNOWN. Every decoupling, converter and ESD-clamp ground pad gets its own via at the pad, and every high-current ground terminal its own via on the current's path (`references/pcb-rules/general.md` GEN-05); every other SMD ground pad one where it fits. A solid plane under every switching loop and fast signal, no tracks on the plane layer, no slots under fast signals, no islands (`scripts/zone_check.py` after every fill). Do not run two long tracks side by side along an edge: they fence off the pour. Check that every power and stitching via really lands in its plane.
12. **Prove a single-point ground** when a chip asks for one. A same-net island: a cut test; in a copy, remove the one bridge, refill, run DRC: the quiet-ground copper must come out unconnected. A net-tie: shorts set as DRC errors, and a check that only the net-tie joins the two grounds' copper. Run the same test on a copy known to be wrong, so the test itself is proven.
13. **No via in an SMD pad, except thermal vias inside an exposed thermal pad as the maker's drawing shows (drill and pitch from the datasheet); keep the paste windows between them, cover them on the far side where the fabricator offers it, and exempt those footprints from the via-to-pad rule by name.** Other vias in pads only as filled via-in-pad ordered on purpose (its terms depend on the layer count: `references/jlcpcb/capabilities.md`).
13b. **Shield cans** (`references/pcb-rules/emc.md` section 10): the ground land under the walls as the can maker draws it; vias from the land to the ground plane all the way round at the pitch of EMC-74; a solid ground plane below the whole shielded area; every net through the wall on an inner layer or through the can's own openings, filtered where it crosses; no surface track under a wall; test points of the nets inside placed outside. Leave the can unfitted where phase 2 said "footprint only".
13c. **Cooling as decided** (`references/pcb-rules/thermal.md`): the heat sink's holes and keep-outs on both sides, nothing tall in its way or in the air's path, parts that dislike heat upstream of the hot ones or at least 10 mm away (starting point), the temperature sensor at the hot part, the fan's connector near the fan. Then recompute the heat table with the copper the board really has (step 18).
14. **No signal track under the switch node, boot copper or inductor on any layer.** The ground plane stays solid under them unless the maker's layout cuts it (a ledger entry). Switch-node copper runs only from the pin to the inductor pad; measure its area. No power or switching track, clock or fast bus under or beside an antenna, a microphone or audio input, a sensor, an ADC input or a crystal, on any layer without a solid ground plane between (`references/pcb-rules/emc.md` section 5).
15. **Test points and connectors:** every point of the schematic's list, placed where a probe reaches it with the board mounted, sized and spaced for the probe (pads of 1.5 mm, 2.54 mm apart, clear of tall parts: `references/pcb-rules/dfm-dft.md` DFM-96), a ground point within reach of each group and one that a clip can hold, each labelled, so the board can be serviced; the points of a production fixture on the side the board definition names (DFM-98); an input-rail point away from fine-pitch pins (`references/pcb-rules/protection-esd.md` ESD-71); a test point never as a stub on a sensitive node (probe that node on a part's pad).
16. **Fill last, in its own process** (`scripts/layout/fill.py`), save, then reload the saved board and check the saved fill again. Draw zones inside the edge clearance. A clean rule check does not prove the saved fill. KiCad's command-line check never fills by itself: it judges the fill as it is saved, and in KiCad 9 it does not report a saved fill that lies too near the board edge; a check started with a refill option, or from a script, fills a copy in memory while the file on disk and the Gerbers keep the older fill. So after every change of copper or of a rule: fill, save, run the rule check, and measure the edge on the exported Gerbers (`scripts/edge_check.py`); `scripts/layout/fill.py --check` compares the saved fill with a fresh one, also on a design the user brought (on a copy). After a change of KiCad version, fill and save again: the two versions fill slightly differently.
17. **Clean up after a router:** acute angles, stubs, loops and unused vias (`scripts/layout/repair.py`), keeping a removal only if no connection opens. It changes the board only with `--in-place`, keeps a step only when the rule check is no worse, and also cleans locked hand copper: read its list.
18. **Write the current and voltage-drop worksheet** with `scripts/neck_check.py`, one row per power path, each named in `[[current.path]]` of `board.toml` (net, worst sustained current, allowed drop and rise); without a named path the result is UNKNOWN:
   - continuous and peak current (the peak is what the protection lets through), the narrowest copper anywhere, resistance cold and hot, drop and loss;
   - the return path, the vias, the connector contacts and the harness count too; compare the voltage at the load's pins with the load's minimum;
   - DRC does not see necks in pours; the script's geometric search does. A neck set by a package's own pad is recorded and gets a thermal check on the bench list;
   - a long power path goes back into the stability and fault simulations with its resistance and inductance.
   - Then fill in the heat table of the design note with what the board really gives each hot part: copper area, vias, and the temperature that follows (`references/pcb-rules/power.md` PWR-62). A regulator on a thin track with two vias passes DRC and fails here.
18b. **Prove the return path of every fast or sensitive net** (`references/pcb-rules/general.md` GEN-19). The nets are listed in `board.toml` under `[fast_nets]` before routing (step 3). After the last fill run `scripts/return_path_check.py`: it measures, per net, the length that runs over a gap in the neighbouring ground layer and the distance from each layer change to the nearest ground via, and draws each plane layer with these nets on top. Open those images and follow each net end to end. Prove the check once on a copy with a slot planted under one net. A board with no such net records that in the ledger. For every line with a target impedance (a USB high-speed pair, an RF line; `[impedance]` in `board.toml`) also run `scripts/impedance_check.py`: it computes the impedance from the width, gap and layer that are really on the board and from the board's layer stack, and compares it with the target. It is a second method beside the manufacturer's calculator, not a replacement; where the two disagree by more than the tolerance, stop and find out why.
19. **One tool process per KiCad file.** Load and save through `scripts/kicad_io.py`, never while the KiCad window has the board open. Parallel trials write to separate files.
20. **Export the 3D model and the interface file again** (`scripts/interface_export.py`), render, and look. Show the user the first render early, as information; the approval comes at gate 2.

## Questions for the user

None. Two cases stop the phase and go to the user: a change to the approved schematic (state the change and its reason, then wait), and a conflict that needs another outline, hole or connector position (go back to phase 8).

## Checks before leaving this phase

- [ ] The footprint table has a row per footprint with the drawing page and a result for pitch, pads, drill, courtyard and pin 1. Evidence: `out/fp_audit.md`.
- [ ] Every part has a 3D model, and every body lies inside its courtyard. Evidence: `out/body_check.md`, or its FAIL "cannot run" carried to phase 10.
- [ ] Every net is in a class; the width, edge, via and silkscreen rules are errors. Evidence: the rule file, and the planted-defect result of each custom rule.
- [ ] DRC with parity on the saved board: 0 errors, 0 unconnected, parity 0. Evidence: `out/drc.json`, `out/drc_summary.txt`.
- [ ] Every ledger rule has an entry: "met" with a measured value or a crop, or "open" with the reason. Evidence: `LAYOUT_RULES_LEDGER.md`.
- [ ] The layout-level EMC measures hold: placement by zones, antenna keep-out and matching network, nothing noisy under or beside a sensitive part. Evidence: a crop per antenna, crystal and sensitive input, against `references/pcb-rules/emc.md` sections 4 to 7.
- [ ] Every supply pin's capacitor is listed with its pad-to-pin distance, the squares of copper in its loop and its own ground via. Evidence: the decoupling table in the ledger.
- [ ] Where a single-point ground exists, the cut test (or, for a net-tie, the short check) proves it, and the control copy fails. Evidence: the two test results.
- [ ] The saved fill, measured after reload, keeps the edge clearance. Evidence: the measured minimum distance.
- [ ] The worksheet covers feed and return of every power path; no neck is under its current; package-limited necks are listed. Evidence: `out/current_worksheet.md`.
- [ ] The build runs again from `src/` to the same board. Evidence: the build log; tracks, vias and zones of the two boards compared equal.
- [ ] The list of parts without a legal label spot exists, even if empty. Evidence: the list in `out/`.
- [ ] The interface file's checksum equals the saved board's; no lock file or tool process is left. Evidence: the checksums; the process and lock-file listing.

## Outputs

- `kicad/<board>.kicad_pcb` (saved and filled), the rule file, the project footprint library; `vendor/jlc_fp/`, `vendor/3d/`
- `src/`: the scripts that build this board
- `LAYOUT_RULES_LEDGER.md`, now with how each rule is met and its evidence
- `out/`: footprint audit, DRC report, zone check, cut test, current worksheet, unlabelled-part list
- `out/3d/<board>.step`, `mech/interface.json`, first renders in `out/review/`
- `PROJECT_STATE.md`: next action phase 10

## Lessons behind this phase

- A supplier footprint had a 2.54 mm pitch for a 2.50 mm connector; on a long part the end pins drifted off and would not insert -> step 1 audits every footprint against the maker's drawing.
- Eight library courtyards were smaller than their pads, so DRC could not see a part placed on them -> step 1 makes the courtyard enclose pads and body.
- Connector 3D models had their origin at pin 1; one body sat millimetres from its pads, over a screw, while every courtyard check passed -> step 2 checks each model's origin, and phase 10 tests bodies, not outlines.
- A fill was saved before the edge rule applied; DRC said 0 while the board file had copper to the outline -> step 16 fills last and re-checks the saved file.
- A chip's quiet ground met the plane at three points because one via poked 0.15 mm past its island -> step 12 proves the single point by a cut test.
- The width check of DRC passed a pour narrowed far below what its current needs -> step 18 measures necks geometrically.
- Routing the long nets first left far fewer gaps than the other order; many labels ended on vias -> steps 7 and 10 fix the order of labels and routes.
- Two tool processes loaded one board while its project file was being rewritten: a dialog on the user's screen and stale locks -> step 19.

The full cases are in `references/lessons.md`.
